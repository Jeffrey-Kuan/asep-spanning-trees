\section{A tree starting with 2222}

The picture shows one tree starting with $2222$. It has seven arrows: two
labeled $A$, two labeled $S$ and three labeled $L$, so its weight is
$A^2S^2L^3$.

%% The "image" environment tells Ximera that what is inside is a picture.
%% It centers the picture and lets us attach alt text with \xmalt (below).
\begin{image}

%% A tikzpicture starts with a list of options in square brackets. Here the
%% options define three STYLES. A style is a named bundle of settings, so that
%% we can write "state" or "arr" later instead of repeating the settings.
%% The form is   name/.style={settings}.
\begin{tikzpicture}[
    %% "state": how one of the eight states (2222, 2233, ...) is written.
    %%    inner sep=2pt   leaves 2pt of empty space around the text. Arrows
    %%                    stop at the edge of this invisible box, so a larger
    %%                    number moves the arrow tips away from the digits.
    %%    font=\large     makes the text a little bigger than normal.
    state/.style={inner sep=2pt, font=\large},
    %% "lab": how a letter next to an arrow (A, S or L) is written.
    lab/.style={font=\large, inner sep=1pt},
    %% "arr": how an arrow is drawn.
    %%    -{Triangle[open, ...]}   a plain start (the "-") and a triangle
    %%                             at the end. "open" means the triangle is an
    %%                             outline and not filled in. length and width
    %%                             set the size of the triangle.
    %%    line width=0.6pt         thickness of the line.
    %%    shorten >=1pt            stop 1pt before the end point, and
    %%    shorten <=1pt            start 1pt after the start point, so the
    %%                             arrow does not touch the digits.
    arr/.style={-{Triangle[open, length=6pt, width=6pt]}, line width=0.6pt, shorten >=1pt, shorten <=1pt},
  ]

  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  %% Step 1. Place the eight states.
  %%
  %%   \node[style] (name) at (x,y) {text};
  %%
  %%   (name)   an internal name, used later to say where arrows start and
  %%            end. It is not printed. Here the name is the same as the text.
  %%   (x,y)    the position of the centre of the text, in centimetres.
  %%            x grows to the right and y grows upward, so the y values here
  %%            are negative and a more negative y is lower on the page.
  %%   {text}   what is printed.
  %%
  %% The positions were measured from the handwritten page, so the states
  %% sit where they do in the drawing. Only the differences between positions
  %% matter: adding the same number to every x, or to every y, moves the whole
  %% picture and changes nothing else.
  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  \node[state] (2222) at (5.3,-4.10) {2222};   % top, the starting state
  \node[state] (3322) at (3.0,-4.85) {3322};   % upper left
  \node[state] (2332) at (5.1,-5.60) {2332};   % middle
  \node[state] (2233) at (7.5,-5.45) {2233};   % upper right
  \node[state] (3232) at (3.2,-6.40) {3232};   % left
  \node[state] (2323) at (7.5,-6.80) {2323};   % right
  \node[state] (3223) at (5.3,-7.25) {3223};   % lower middle
  \node[state] (3333) at (5.4,-8.35) {3333};   % bottom

  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  %% Step 2. Draw the seven arrows, each followed by its letter.
  %%
  %%   \draw[arr] (start) -- (end);
  %%
  %%   "--" means a straight line. The arrowhead is at the end.
  %%
  %%   (2222.south east) means the lower right corner of the invisible box
  %%   around the text of node 2222. These corner and side names are called
  %%   anchors. The ones used here are
  %%        north = top side        south = bottom side
  %%        east  = right side      west  = left side
  %%   and the four corners: north east, north west, south east, south west.
  %%   Choosing the anchor chooses the side of the text the arrow leaves from
  %%   or arrives at.
  %%
  %%   The letter is a separate node placed by hand at (x,y), near the middle
  %%   of the arrow. The dollar signs put the letter in math italics.
  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

  % Arrow from 2222 to 2233, labeled A.
  % It leaves the lower right corner of 2222 and arrives at the upper left
  % corner of 2233.
  \draw[arr] (2222.south east) -- (2233.north west);
  \node[lab] at (6.85,-4.15) {$A$};

  % Arrow from 2233 to 2323, labeled L. Straight down: bottom of 2233 to top
  % of 2323. The L sits to the right of the arrow.
  \draw[arr] (2233.south) -- (2323.north);
  \node[lab] at (8.0,-6.10) {$L$};

  % Arrow from 2323 to 2332, labeled L. It goes up and to the left.
  \draw[arr] (2323.north west) -- (2332.south east);
  \node[lab] at (6.2,-6.55) {$L$};

  % Arrow from 2332 to 3232, labeled L. It goes down and to the left.
  \draw[arr] (2332.south west) -- (3232.north east);
  \node[lab] at (4.15,-5.55) {$L$};

  % Arrow from 3333 to 3223, labeled S. Straight up: top of 3333 to bottom of
  % 3223.
  \draw[arr] (3333.north) -- (3223.south);
  \node[lab] at (5.75,-7.80) {$S$};

  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  %% The two long arrows are curves, not straight lines.
  %%
  %%   \draw[arr] (start) .. controls (P) and (Q) .. (end);
  %%
  %%   This draws a smooth curve from start to end. P and Q are two "control
  %%   points". The curve does not pass through them. It leaves the start
  %%   heading toward P, and it arrives at the end coming from the direction
  %%   of Q. Moving P or Q further away makes the curve bulge out more.
  %%   The easiest way to adjust a curve is to change P and Q a little and
  %%   recompile.
  %% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

  % Arrow from 2233 to 3333, labeled A: the long curve on the right.
  % It leaves the right side of 2233, heads right and down toward (9.3,-6.2),
  % and comes into the right side of 3333 from the direction of (8.6,-8.3).
  \draw[arr] (2233.east) .. controls (9.3,-6.2) and (8.6,-8.3) .. (3333.east);
  \node[lab] at (8.45,-8.00) {$A$};

  % Arrow from 3333 to 3322, labeled S: the long curve on the left.
  % It leaves the left side of 3333, heads left toward (1.6,-8.7), and comes
  % up into the left side of 3322 from the direction of (1.4,-5.9).
  \draw[arr] (3333.west) .. controls (1.6,-8.7) and (1.4,-5.9) .. (3322.west);
  \node[lab] at (2.45,-8.50) {$S$};
\end{tikzpicture}

\section{A tree starting with 2332}

The picture shows one tree starting with $2332$. It also has seven arrows:
two labeled $A$, two labeled $S$ and three labeled $L$, so its weight is
$A^2S^2L^3$.

\begin{image}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
    % The same three styles as in the first picture. Styles defined inside
    % one tikzpicture are not remembered by the next, so they are repeated.
    state/.style={inner sep=2pt, font=\large},
    lab/.style={font=\large, inner sep=1pt},
    arr/.style={-{Triangle[open, length=6pt, width=6pt]}, line width=0.6pt, shorten >=1pt, shorten <=1pt},

  % Step 1. The eight states, placed as in the handwritten page.
  \node[state] (2332) at (5.5,-3.25) {2332};   % top, the starting state

  \node[state] (3322) at (4.2,-5.45) {3322};   % left, below 3232
  \node[state] (2233) at (7.95,-5.40) {2233};  % right

  \node[lab] at (4.25,-2.85) {$L$};

  % 3232 to 3322, labeled L: down and to the right.
  \draw[arr] (3232.south) -- (3322.north west);
  \node[lab] at (2.95,-4.75) {$L$};

  % 3322 to 2222, labeled S: up and to the right.
  \draw[arr] (3322.north east) -- (2222.south west);
  \node[lab] at (5.45,-5.40) {$S$};

  % 2222 to 2233, labeled A: to the right and down.
  \draw[arr] (2222.east) -- (2233.north west);
  \node[lab] at (7.05,-4.50) {$A$};

  % 2233 to 2323, labeled L: up and to the right.
  \draw[arr] (2233.north) -- (2323.south);
  \node[lab] at (8.95,-4.60) {$L$};

  % 3333 to 3223, labeled S: straight up.
  \draw[arr] (3333.north) -- (3223.south);
  \node[lab] at (6.35,-7.05) {$S$};

  % 2233 to 3333, labeled A: the one curved arrow. It leaves the bottom of
  % 2233 heading down toward (8.0,-6.9) and comes into the right side of 3333

  \draw[arr] (2233.south) .. controls (8.0,-6.9) and (7.5,-7.6) .. (3333.east);
  \node[lab] at (8.05,-7.25) {$A$};
\end{tikzpicture}
